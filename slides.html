D3Deliverable 3 · definitions

One page. Four definitions.

Answers: ARR basis / PAYGO transition date + netting / overage / net new — one page

1 · ARR basis

Recognised ARR, spot: MAX(usage, commit ÷ 12) × 12, latest complete month.

Why: it is what billing invoices, so it reconciles on 520 of 520 accounts. It equals src_usage_monthly.total_billed_usd × 12 exactly.

Not “commit plus overage” — that holds on only 147 of 520 rows. For the 31 accounts consuming below commit it double-counts.

2 · PAYGO → commit transition

Date: close date, not creation date.

Netted against: trailing-3-month recognised ARR on that account.

Why close, not creation: creation-date netting credits the deal with PAYGO growth between creation and close that the deal did not cause — ACC-0239 alone is $126K.

The honest problem: trailing-3 is right for scoring what a deal added at signing. The bridge measures ARR at 31 Oct, and by then 21 accounts clear their new commit on usage alone — so $545,238 is counted twice, a 5.1% overstatement. Live driver: ASSUMPTIONS!B25.

3 · Overage inside a committed account

In ARR, as its own layer.

Why: the floor cannot move inside a quarter — a contract is signed and locked. Overage moves daily. Separating them is what lets you see which part of net new is a signature and which is consumption, because different teams drive them.

Consequence: $5.71M/yr of recognised ARR across 31 accounts is unconsumed commit. Contractually real, and the down-round exposure at renewal.

4 · Net new ARR in this forecast

Ending recognised ARR less opening recognised ARR, decomposed into: consumption growth, incremental commit (netted, stage-weighted), and new logos.

Renewals are excluded deliberately: a sweep converts overage into commit. Quality changes, amount does not. Upland Research already recognises $5.66M on usage against a $2.12M commit.

Churn and contraction are not separate lines. Churn is $9,225 across 23 months — not a line item. Contraction is real (0.40–0.54 per $1 of expansion) but embedded in the segment growth rate, which is why Enterprise nets to +0.02% with 6 up and 8 down. I cannot stress it independently. First thing I would build.

Answers: What to ask instead, what reps can know, how it feeds the model

what we ask todaychangewhat reps can realistically know
Gross commit on the paperkeepThey know the contract. Finance needs it.

Claim this slide must landTwo principles. Never ask a rep for a number the system already holds. And ask only what they uniquely know — whether consumption is durable, and when it starts.

How it feeds the model: the durability answer becomes a per-account haircut on the consumption layer — the 58.1% nobody currently forecasts. The start-date answer moves commit between Q3 and Q4. The renewal answer populates a contraction line the model does not yet have. Each new question maps to a layer that is currently unforecast.
