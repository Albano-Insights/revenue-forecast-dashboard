01ARR basis and netting

The prompt says no basis is prescribed and several defensible ones disagree. These four decisions set everything downstream.

ARR basis
Recognised ARR, spot · total_billed_usd × 12
Usage-only  billed-commit-only  trailing average
ASSUMPTIONS!B5 · WORKING F, G, P
It is what the customer is actually billed, so it reconciles to cash without a second model. Usage-only ignores the commit floor and undercounts every committed account; billed-commit-only ignores overage, which is where the growth is. Spot, not trailing, because plan is stated as a point-in-time target.
Netting date
Close date
Creation date
ASSUMPTIONS!B6
PAYGO growth that happens while a deal is being worked was not created by the deal. The prompt's own ACC-0239 example is worth $126K of difference. Netting at creation also reverses on declining accounts and undercounts there, so it is wrong in both directions.
Netting base
Trailing 3-month recognised ARR
Usage run-rate  single prior month
src_opportunities!T
Recognised, not usage — netting against usage double-counts the commit floor on an account already billing at it. Three months rather than one because a single month on a consumption business is noise. Corrected during the build: divide by the months the account actually has, not by three, or a month before the account existed counts as $0 and inflates incremental. Six Q3 opportunities sit on accounts that started mid-window.
Overage
Own layer inside ARR · MAX(usage, commit/12)
Excluded as non-recurring
WORKING P, AF
The MAX is the mechanism that enforces one dollar, one home. It partitions the change, not the level: a dollar is either consumption growth or incremental commit, never both. Excluding overage would drop the fastest-growing part of the book.

02The September problem

Half a month of data drives the single most sensitive number in the model.

Partial-month extrapolation
Weekday-weighted factor · 2.0004
Naive ×2  days elapsed / days in month
CALC_SEP · ASSUMPTIONS!B15
Worth exactly nothing this month — Sep 1–15 and full September share the same weekday mix, so it equals ×2 to four decimals. Kept because the forecast reruns weekly and on every other day of the month naive scaling is wrong by up to 12%. The validation is the deliverable: −0.4% on July, +0.1% on August.
Growth measurement basis
Daily table to daily table
Daily September vs monthly August
CALC_GROWTH
My own error, caught mid-build. Grossing September up by the 95.8% coverage factor while taking August from the monthly table inflated Enterprise from +0.0% to +4.4%. Like-for-like restores blended +2.50%.
October carry
1.00 — September's rate repeats
0.00 flat  revert to trailing mean
ASSUMPTIONS!B24 — a toggle, not a constant
Lag-1 autocorrelation of monthly growth is +0.04 blended, −0.11 Enterprise, so carrying one month forward is judgement, not measurement. Carry 0.00 gives 53% of plan; 1.00 gives 59%. Since trailing-3 historical growth is +12.1% against September's +2.5%, 1.00 is the conservative end of the range, not the middle — the model is anchored on the weakest month in two years.
Growth rate grain
One rate per segment
Account-level, trailing 3 months  capped ±20%/mo  account-level for the top 25
CALC_GROWTH · ASSUMPTIONS!B10:B12 · backtest_grain.py
Proposed, tested, rejected. Rolling 2-month-ahead backtest over 14 windows: the segment rate lands at median +0.03% error, MAE 10.74%, 50% of windows over — unbiased. Blending toward an account rate degrades it monotonically, reaching +8.90% median, MAE 13.94%, 79% over at full weight. The mechanism is compounding: a per-account rate has unbounded upside and a downside floored at −100%, so individually unbiased rates aggregate into an upward-biased book. Capping at ±20%/mo recovers the accuracy (MAE 10.61%) but buys 0.13 points at the cost of a real directional bias — a bad trade for a number that goes to a board. The counterintuitive result: account-level on the largest accounts was the worst variant of all (MAE 13.88%, 79% over). The top 10 are 50.7% of the book, so their rate noise is the book rather than something that diversifies away. Account-level growth stays in the dashboard for targeting, where it is fine, and out of the forecast, where it is not. The live question is not the grain, it is the window — see October carry above: Enterprise reads +0.02%/mo on September against +11.61%/mo on the trailing three, on 69% of the book.

06Data problems found

The prompt says the documented ones are in the dictionary and there may be others. These are the others.

  • 31 of 270Opportunities carry a creation date after the 16 Sep as-of date, running to March 2027. Silently inflates any creation-rate calculation.
  • 281 of 378Accounts move by exactly −0.32% in September. The generator gave most of the book a deterministic flat month, so per-account consumption modelling below the top ~100 accounts is uninformative.
  • 10 of 39New logos in the last six months appear as a closed-won CRM opportunity. Three quarters of new business never touches the pipeline.
  • 39 of 270Opportunities record a region that differs from their owning rep's region. Any region cut depends on which field you pick.
  • 14 : 2Won to lost on deals already resolved this quarter — an 87.5% win rate against 46% historically. Suggests losses are recorded late, so open pipeline contains deals that are already dead.
  • 0 repsSelf-Serve carries a $398K Q3 plan and 320 accounts with no owner. Not a data error exactly, but nobody is on the hook for it.
  • 2.00 vs 2.99Headcount plan assumes hires that have not happened. Actual AEs are 2 per cell everywhere; plan assumes up to 2.99 productive by October.
  • Ramp conflictTwo reps are flagged Ramping in reps.csv but are past month four under the stated 0/.33/.67/1.00 convention.

07Questions I expect, and the answer

"59% seems pessimistic. Are you sure?"
The opposite risk is larger. The model anchors October on September, the weakest month in two years, and monthly growth does not autocorrelate. Every historical estimator says October should be +12% to +21%. I am at the conservative end deliberately, and the toggle is one cell.
"Your Discovery win rate is 44%. That is far too high."
Agreed, and it is what the data says — no opportunity in the snapshot history ever exits before Negotiation. That is why I do not lean on stage. The discriminator I actually use is close date against the 101-day cycle.
"The field is calling $6.73M. You are saying $10.6M. Why is that not agreement?"
Different units. Theirs is gross commit ARR; $5.66M of the Q3 gross pipeline is revenue already in the book under a different label. In net-new terms their call is $2.30M. The comparison everyone is making is not a comparison.
"What would you build first?"
The ARR-basis table at account × month, with the MAX applied once and stored. Every other question becomes a query against it. Point-in-time pipeline snapshots second, because without them you cannot measure forecast accuracy at all.
